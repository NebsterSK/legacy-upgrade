My background is backend, so front-end design is the part I purposely work on. I watch two YouTube channels about it, Cole Jain and Sajid, and a while ago I decided Claude should have the same input I do. I pulled the captions from the top 10 videos of each channel with yt-dlp CLI, had Claude find the principles that kept coming back, and turned that into my own frontend-design skill.

Then I tested it properly. One goal, redesign my own website. Three skills, three git branches, three results: mine, Anthropic's frontend-design skill, and Impeccable.

🥉 Mine came last. Not even close.

The other two were built by people who do this for a living and refined over far longer than one evening of transcripts. My version had read about design principles. Theirs knew what to do with them.

The lesson is not that the idea was stupid, because pulling knowledge into a skill does work and I would do it again for something genuinely mine. The lesson is that I assumed mine would win because it was built from my own sources. It did not.

It has never been cheaper or faster for me to build whatever comes to mind. Cheap and fast is not the same as good.
