Most people expect a website to be a one time payment. Build it, pay the invoice, done.

It never is. Something leaves your account every month either way, and how much depends entirely on how the site was built.

🌐 Some of it is unavoidable, and any decent agency will tell you about it up front. A domain is somewhere around 15 euros a year, hosting for a small business site is a few euros a month. That is the cost of being "on the internet", and it is small enough to stop thinking about.

💶 The rest depends on what the site was built on. On a no code platform you pay a monthly plan, and the features you end up needing tend to live in the higher tiers. On WordPress the software itself is free, but the premium theme, the page builder and the handful of plugins holding it together usually are not, and most of those renew every year. On top of either, there is someone to call every time you need a change the editor cannot do.

On a bank statement those two piles look identical. They are not. One keeps your site online. The other is rent on software that belongs to somebody else, and four years of it adds up to a number worth knowing.

🤝 Here is how I handle it. If you paid for it, it is yours. Contract or no contract. If you want to work with someone else later, you get the code, the credentials, and ownership of the hosting and the domain. That is not generosity. A client who stays because leaving would be painful is not really a client, and I would rather keep people because the work is good than because the exit is expensive.
